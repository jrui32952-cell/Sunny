Brand Concept

The cars on the track and the outsiders are like a hook in the middle — hooked into the hearts of both sides.

The Insight

The initial research phase surfaced a fundamental tension within Formula One's audience: casual fans drawn to spectacle, and engineers who treat every corner as a calculation. The brand's opportunity lay not in choosing one, but in articulating the invisible thread between them — the driver on the track is inseparable from the team behind the data.

That's why the only way to make it a true motorsports brand is to capture both sides: the driver on the track and the team behind him. The hook hooks them together — the driver's failure is the team's regret; the data staff's error drags the driver down. They are literally, physically, one.

The Symbol Logic

I chose the tire as the symbol's foundation because it is the first element to communicate automobile industry at a glance — and in Formula One, the tire is the most critical variable. I wanted the mark to exaggerate dynamic effects, not replicate a static tire.

A continuous curve of the tire silhouette in motion — thick and thin lines, dramatic speed differentials — creates a mark that people can intuitively feel before they read it: danger, challenge, agitation, momentum. The font is heavily slanted and irregular, symbolising extreme passion and excitement.

Logo Anatomy

One mark.
Three meanings.
One racing soul.

Tire in Motion
The swirling circular element evokes a tire at speed — the contact patch that connects the car to the track. Thick-to-thin line weight creates the sensation of blur and velocity.
The Hook Itself
The dominant sweeping gesture is the hook — representing the overtaking move on track, the act of claiming position. Aggressive, decisive, forward-leaning.
Team Unity
Hooks lock together. The form implies connection between driver and crew, team and nation, constructor and championship. A single mark that carries a collective metaphor.

Design Process

From tire sketches
to racing mark.

In my first sketches, I was interested in using words to design racing graphics — creating a direct connection between company name and car. But I realised this was one-dimensional. I needed to identify the ideas connected to the race before creating the word, and diversify outward rather than one-to-one.

Phase 01 · Research
Tire Photography

Collected close-up and long-distance tire photos from F1 official websites, comparing with-movement and without-movement. Key finding: a static replica of a tire pattern does not bring out emotion — only an explosive, moving image does.

Phase 02 · Sketching
12+ Logo Concepts

Multiple concepts explored different approaches to the tire motif — orbital, angled, 3D perspective, abstracted, and combined with wordmark in various configurations. Narrow to bold, italic to upright, complex to simple.

Phase 03 · Refinement
Colour Exploration

15+ colour variations tested across warm (orange, red, pink) and cool (blue, teal, purple) palettes. The teacher's principle applied: more than three colours in a logo creates distraction. Warm + bright wins for motorsport impression.

Phase 04 · Final
Identity System

Orange (#dc7216) on orange background — primary lockup. Teal (#34aaa7) as secondary pairing. Symbol in multiple scales (large/medium/small), vertical and horizontal lockups tested and standardised against the sizing template.

Typography & Wordmark
Hook
Typographic Rationale

I prefer a font with a lot of slant and irregularity — symbolising sports, extreme passion, and excitement. The slant carries forward momentum without needing an image. The partial small cap treatment of the "H" creates a distinctive character: not fully uppercase (too rigid), not fully lowercase (too casual) — occupying exactly the tension zone the brand lives in.

Multiple typeface explorations were conducted: heavy condensed sans, calligraphic scripts, extreme italics, speed-line distortions. The final selection balances legibility at distance with personality at close range — a requirement unique to motorsport applications.

Reflection

Strategic Outcomes

On Concept
  • The brief demanded a multi-dimensional translation: from a single word (speed) to a compound of emotion, culture, physics, and visual form. The mark encodes all four simultaneously.
  • The hook metaphor — binding driver to team — emerged through audience research and technical interviews, not intuition. Strategic context generated the concept where visual brainstorming alone could not.
  • A literal replica of the tyre communicates category but not character. An explosive, dynamic reinterpretation communicates speed, danger, and identity in a single mark.
  • The colour system was deliberately constrained to two primaries. In motorsport environments — livery, signage, broadcast — restraint amplifies, not limits, recognition.
On Execution
  • Thick-to-thin line variation encodes perceived velocity without animation — the eye processes motion before the brain reads the form. This is kineticism as brand language.
  • The wordmark's slant and irregular weight distribution operate as a second emotional signal, independent of the symbol. Typography carries personality; the two reinforce rather than repeat each other.
  • Colour testing across 15+ palettes established that warm-bright combinations outperform cool neutrals in motorsport brand recognition — a decision validated empirically, not assumed aesthetically.
  • The next phase will validate the mark kinetically — Cinema 4D animation to test system integrity at race speed across three-dimensional livery and environmental signage.
