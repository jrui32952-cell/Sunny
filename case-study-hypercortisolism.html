Hypercortisolism

01 · Context

Stress is invisible until the body responds.

How can an installation make an internal condition physically understandable?

Chronic stress is often described through abstract language and medical data. This project reframes that information as an embodied encounter: touching the installation completes a circuit, raises a cortisol gauge, and reveals a symptom through immediate visual feedback.

02 · Interaction Ritual

The participant completes the system.

The interaction is deliberately simple so attention stays on the body. Each action connects physical contact to a visible consequence.

01

Approach

The participant encounters a hexagonal body map and a screen-based cortisol gauge.

02

Touch

A hand reaches toward a symptom zone while the other contact maintains the EARTH connection.

03

Complete

The participant’s body closes the hidden conductive circuit and becomes part of the interface.

04

Reveal

The gauge responds and the selected symptom becomes visible, connecting action to physiological effect.

03 · Five Symptom Gestures

Each zone turns a clinical effect into a location the participant can reach, touch and remember.

04 · Key Decision

Make the body functional—not decorative.

  • Rejected: pressing a conventional button to trigger health information.
  • Chosen: requiring the participant’s body to close the circuit.
  • Why it matters: the interaction form and subject become the same idea—the body both receives stress and activates its explanation.

05 · Reflection

Embodiment changes how information is remembered.

The strongest part of the project is not the sensor or the screen in isolation. It is the interaction contract between them: the participant must physically enter the system before information appears. That ritual makes an invisible biological process tangible.

A future iteration would test the clarity of each symptom gesture with participants, refine the conductive zones for more reliable contact, and compare the installation’s learning impact with a screen-only explanation.
