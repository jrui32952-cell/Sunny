Design Statement

Voice is not a trigger — it is a force.

The Question

Interaction design usually treats sound as a command — a button press made audible. What if we inverted that relationship? What if the microphone became a force transducer, and the user's voice became a physical field capable of transforming material states?


Ice cream served as the ideal interaction medium: familiar, affectionate, universally understood — and subject to three distinct state changes. Smash (solid rupture), Melt (liquid flow), Freeze (crystallisation). Each state has its own physics. Each physics has its own relationship to sound.

The Framework

Four sound dimensions from lecture structured the design logic: physiological (body-as-input — the microphone reads the user's physical presence), cognitive (visual causality — the scoop explodes because you screamed, and you understand this instantly), emotional (the ice cream is a comfort object, held under vocal stress), and behavioural (each mode shapes a distinct interaction loop through unique physics parameters).


The goal was not to build a fun toy. It was to build a precise instrument — one that made the relationship between sound and physical change both legible and expressive.

Interaction System

Three modes.
Three physical laws.

Same P5.js engine. Same ice cream form. Three completely different physics tables — each one mapping a different relationship between the user's voice and the material state of the scoop.

Mode 01
Smash It
Yell, clap, or scream at it

Solid rupture — voice energy explodes the scoop outward radially. The louder the sound, the further each of the 65 circles flies from the origin. Spring physics pulls them back when silence returns. The interaction contract is explosive and instant — cause and effect are simultaneous.

boom force 32 · spring 0.078 · radial displacement · instantaneous response
Mode 02
Melt It
Heat melts everything

Liquid flow — sustained sound accumulates as heat, causing the scoop to droop downward over time. meltDroop increases cumulatively. Stop talking and the droop slows — but it doesn't immediately reverse. The contract is cumulative and irreversible — sound leaves a physical trace.

meltDroop accumulation · gravity sag · heat waves · lerp 0.016 → 0.04
Mode 03
Freeze It
Crystallise it with sound

Inversion — silence turns colour to grey; sound restores it. The scoop is permanently frozen in stasis. Only your voice can bring colour back — but the moment you stop, it crystallises again. The contract is inverted — silence is the active state, sound is the interruption.

colorAmt lerp · grayVal 125 · silence = change · inversion logic

Conceptual Framework

Artist References

Two works defined the conceptual territory — not as stylistic references, but as structural models for how sound becomes physical transformation.

Artist Reference 01
Bill Viola
The Messenger, 1996
Body as Transformation Site

Viola treats the body as a site of physical state change. This project inherits the logic: the ice cream form undergoes transformation driven by vocal energy.

Looping as Interaction Structure

The Messenger has no fixed beginning or end. Sound of Melting similarly has no scripted sequence — the user enters, disturbs the form, and the system returns to stillness.

Sound as Physical Force

Volume is not metaphor — it is vector force applied to position. amplitude.getLevel() becomes the boom variable that pushes geometry outward.

Artist Reference 02
Granular-Synthesis
Modell 5, 1994
Granular Architecture

The ice cream scoop is built from 65 overlapping semi-transparent circles. Form emerges from collective overlap — no individual circle IS the scoop. Directly from Hentschläger & Langheinrich.

Intensity as Aesthetic Goal

High amplitude is the aesthetic condition aimed toward. A whisper → gentle displacement; a scream → maximum radial explosion. Volume is intention, not incidence.

Repetition with Variation

Each burst particle follows identical physics rules but departs at a unique angle — never identical across two interactions, yet always unmistakably the same system.

Challenges & Solutions

Four problems.
Four precise fixes.

Challenge 01
Inverted Cone Geometry
Bottom wider than top
The thirteen layer rectangles were drawn with widths proportional to index from scoop downward — but index direction was reversed. The cone appeared upside-down.
Inverted the layer index so layer 0 (top) is widest, layer 12 narrowest. Rewrote xOffset = (maxWidth - layerWidth)/2.
Challenge 02
Smash Particles Too Compact
Effect not visible
Initial burst produced a tight cluster. All 65 circles displaced by the same small radial force (set to 8) — indistinguishable from the resting state. The explosion was invisible.
Increased boom force from 8 → 32, applied directionally (dx/d × force). Spring coefficient tuned 0.12 → 0.078 to slow the return and sustain the explosion.
Challenge 03
Hint Text Off-Screen
cy − R × 1.42 renders below zero
Hint positioned at cy − R×1.42 produced y < 0 on all canvas sizes. No visual error thrown when drawString renders outside bounds — silent failure on every viewport.
Multiplier changed 1.42 → 0.62. Hint lifetime extended 7s → 12s. Threshold raised 0.012 → 0.018 to avoid false triggers in quiet rooms.
Challenge 04
Freeze Scoop Invisible
grayVal=231 too close to background
Frozen desaturation (grayVal=231) was too close to Mode 3's pale blue background. The core interaction contract — silence=grey / sound=colour — was completely unreadable.
grayVal reduced to 125 (mid-grey). colorAmt lerp adjusted so micLevel 0.010–0.030 produces a noticeable colour shift. Contrast restored; contract legible.
Key Decision

Why silence became the design breakthrough

Smash and Melt both followed the intuitive model: sound = change. Louder = more effect. This is the expected interaction contract — it feels satisfying because it confirms our physical intuitions about force.

Freeze inverted this entirely. The discovery came from a negative space: what if the active state was silence? What if sound was the interruption, not the cause? The frozen scoop crystallises in stillness and momentarily recovers colour only when you speak — then crystallises again the moment you stop.

The Freeze mode's inversion logic — silence produces visual change, not sound — was the most conceptually significant discovery of the project.

Reflection

What I Learned

Technical
  • P5.js audio pipeline: AudioIn, Amplitude, FFT, and the browser gesture unlock requirement for Web Audio API.
  • Physics-based Mover class: applyForce(), spring return, and voice-driven vector displacement as a design tool.
  • Multi-mode state management: one sketch.js, three completely different behaviours via lookup tables — not conditional spaghetti.
  • Debugging real-time audio: inaudible thresholds, off-screen element rendering failures, contrast on live canvas.
  • Amplitude smoothing at 0.50 adds ~100ms latency — a tradeoff between responsiveness and noise sensitivity.
Design
  • Sound is a design material with the same specificity as colour or form — it can be tuned, calibrated, and choreographed.
  • The three interaction contracts (explosive / cumulative / inversion) proved that a single physical medium can support radically different emotional registers.
  • Parameter shift is both technical and aesthetic — inseparable from code. The spring constant is also a feeling.
  • The landing page IS interaction design: mode cards reduce pre-audio cognitive load and prime the user's physical behaviour before they interact.
  • Next: Melt-to-Freeze transition (silence after full melt triggers recrystallisation), mobile gesture input, and FFT band analysis for pitch-specific effects.
